# Managing Academic and Professional Stress and Burnout

## 1. Recognizing Burnout vs Everyday Fatigue
Burnout is a state of emotional, physical, and mental exhaustion caused by excessive and prolonged stress. It occurs when you feel overwhelmed, emotionally drained, and unable to meet constant demands.
Key signs include:
- Chronic fatigue and low physical energy.
- Cynicism, detachment, and emotional numbness toward tasks you previously enjoyed.
- A pervasive sense of ineffectiveness and reduced accomplishment.
- Difficulty making decisions and brain fog.

## 2. Micro-Rest and Task Chunking (The 15-Minute Rule)
When facing intense academic or workload paralysis, the brain's executive functioning is impaired by cortisol.
Effective recovery steps:
- Task Chunking: Break tasks down into tiny, non-threatening micro-steps (e.g., opening a document and typing one headline, rather than "write the paper").
- The 15-Minute Rule: Commit to doing an activity for just 15 minutes. After 15 minutes, give yourself explicit permission to stop or take a 5-minute break without guilt.
- Strategic Micro-Rests: Step away from screens for 3 minutes every hour, look at distant horizon points, and stretch your spine.

## 3. Cognitive Reframing and Boundary Setting
Burnout frequently feeds on catastrophic thinking (e.g., "If I don't finish everything perfectly, I have failed").
- Gentle reframing: Shift from "I must do everything now" to "I will do what is sustainable today. Progress is more valuable than perfection."
- Say no to non-essential obligations to protect your mental baseline. Rest is not a reward you earn after exhaustion; it is a biological requirement.
