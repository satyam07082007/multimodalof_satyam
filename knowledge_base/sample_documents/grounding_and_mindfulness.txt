# Evidence-Based Grounding and Mindfulness Protocols

## 1. The 5-4-3-2-1 Sensory Grounding Technique
The 5-4-3-2-1 grounding method is a clinically validated cognitive exercise designed to bring awareness back to the present moment during acute anxiety or distress.
How to practice:
- Notice 5 things you can see around you (e.g., a pen on the desk, light reflecting on a wall, a plant).
- Notice 4 things you can physically feel or touch (e.g., the texture of your sweater, your feet pressing firmly against the floor).
- Notice 3 distinct sounds you can hear (e.g., distant traffic, hum of an appliance, birds outside).
- Notice 2 things you can smell (or two scents you appreciate, like coffee or fresh air).
- Notice 1 thing you can taste (or take a slow sip of cool water and notice the sensation).

## 2. Box Breathing (Square Breathing)
Box breathing is a powerful autonomic nervous system regulation tool used to reduce stress hormones and activate the parasympathetic "rest and digest" state.
Steps:
1. Inhale slowly through your nose for a count of 4.
2. Hold your breath gently for a count of 4.
3. Exhale smoothly through your mouth for a count of 4.
4. Pause and hold empty for a count of 4.
Repeat for 4 to 6 cycles. Keep shoulders soft and unclenched.

## 3. Progressive Muscle Relaxation (PMR)
Progressive muscle relaxation involves intentionally tensing specific muscle groups for 5 seconds and then releasing them for 15 seconds while observing the contrast between tension and relaxation.
Sequence:
1. Toes and feet: Curl toes downward, hold, then release.
2. Calves and thighs: Tighten leg muscles, hold, release.
3. Hands and arms: Clench fists, hold, release.
4. Shoulders and neck: Lift shoulders toward ears, hold, then let them drop completely.
