# Navigating Acute Anxiety and Panic De-escalation

## 1. Physiological Sigh (Fast Nervous System De-escalation)
The physiological sigh was studied at Stanford as one of the fastest biological mechanisms to bring down elevated heart rate and soothe acute hyperarousal.
Protocol:
1. Take a deep inhale through your nose.
2. At the very top of the inhale, take a second short, sharp 'sniff' of air to fully expand collapsed alveoli in the lungs.
3. Exhale slowly, fully, and smoothly through your mouth for twice as long as the inhale.
Repeating 2 to 3 physiological sighs triggers the vagus nerve and immediately lowers cardiovascular agitation.

## 2. The R.A.I.N. Mindfulness Protocol
Developed by Tara Brach and mindfulness clinicians for difficult emotions:
- R — Recognize what is happening: Acknowledge the anxious thought or feeling without pushing it away ("I am feeling fear right now").
- A — Allow the experience to be there: Rather than fighting the physical sensations (racing heart, tight chest), permit them to exist as temporary body sensations.
- I — Investigate with kindness: Ask yourself with curiosity: "Where am I holding this tension in my body? What does this vulnerable part of me need right now?"
- N — Natural awareness / Non-identification: Realize that you are the conscious witness of the anxiety, not the anxiety itself. You are greater than any passing emotion.

## 3. Cognitive Decatastrophizing
When anxious thoughts spiral, ask yourself three grounding questions:
1. What is the absolute worst that could realistically happen?
2. What is the most likely, realistic outcome?
3. If the worst happened, what is one concrete step I could take to handle it?
Writing answers down externalizes the fear and relieves cognitive overload.
